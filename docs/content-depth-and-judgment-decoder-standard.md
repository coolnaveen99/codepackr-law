# CodePackr Law — Content Depth & Judgment Decoding Standard

**Purpose:** Prevent shallow topic content and establish a measurable educational depth standard for all existing and future CodePackr Law topics.

## 1. Core principle

There is no single academically correct word count for every law topic. A one-section definition, a major constitutional doctrine, a procedural mechanism and a landmark judgment naturally require different depth.

Therefore CodePackr Law uses **minimum content-depth bands**, not a fixed universal word count.

The objective is not to make pages unnecessarily long. The objective is to ensure that a student can:

1. understand the concept;
2. identify the governing law;
3. deconstruct the rule;
4. identify the legal issue;
5. understand how courts reason;
6. apply the law to facts;
7. distinguish neighbouring concepts;
8. remember leading authorities;
9. answer an exam/problem question;
10. connect the topic to practical legal work.

IRAC and case-briefing guidance consistently emphasizes issue, rule, application/reasoning and conclusion/holding, while case-brief guidance also identifies facts, procedural history and the court's reasoning as essential learning components. The ABA specifically describes reasoning as the bridge between black-letter law and facts. citeturn0search2turn0search14

# 2. Topic depth bands

## Band A — Foundation / Definition Topic

Use for:
- simple definitions;
- introductory concepts;
- basic legal terminology;
- narrow non-controversial procedural concepts.

### Minimum target

**1,200–1,800 words** of substantive student-facing content.

Must contain:

- definition;
- purpose;
- statutory/legal source;
- essential elements;
- simple example;
- boundary/exception;
- at least 1 relevant authority where appropriate;
- common misconception;
- practical relevance;
- revision points.

Do not inflate content merely to meet the word target.

---

## Band B — Standard Doctrinal / Section Topic

Use for:
- ordinary statutory sections;
- doctrines;
- remedies;
- procedural provisions;
- recurring law-school topics.

### Minimum target

**1,800–3,000 words** of substantive content.

Required:

1. Executive explanation.
2. Legal definition.
3. Historical/provenance context where relevant.
4. Statutory text/deconstruction.
5. Ingredients/elements.
6. Exceptions/provisos.
7. Rule/test.
8. Application to facts.
9. At least 2 illustrations.
10. At least 3 relevant authorities where available.
11. Distinction from neighbouring concepts.
12. Common mistakes.
13. Practical/courtroom relevance.
14. Revision points.
15. At least one problem/hypothetical.

---

## Band C — Core / High-Value Topic

Use for:
- major statutory provisions;
- frequently examined doctrines;
- important procedural mechanisms;
- major remedies;
- major constitutional principles;
- topics with substantial case law.

### Minimum target

**3,000–5,000 words** of substantive content.

Required:

- all Band B requirements;
- deeper statutory deconstruction;
- constituent elements;
- burden of proof;
- procedural stages;
- jurisdiction;
- limitation where applicable;
- competing interpretations;
- 5–8 significant authorities where sufficient authorities exist;
- ratio/holding/relevance for each important case;
- at least 2 detailed hypotheticals;
- counter-arguments;
- practical drafting implications;
- exam/problem-solving framework;
- current-law verification.

---

## Band D — Major / Landmark Topic

Use for:
- constitutional doctrines;
- major landmark provisions;
- foundational criminal/civil doctrines;
- topics with extensive jurisprudential development;
- topics connecting multiple statutes.

### Minimum target

**5,000–8,000+ words** where the subject genuinely warrants it.

Required:

- full doctrinal history;
- statutory/constitutional framework;
- competing judicial approaches;
- evolution of the doctrine;
- important conflicting authorities;
- later treatment;
- procedural implications;
- evidentiary implications;
- comparative distinctions;
- multiple factual applications;
- research references;
- detailed judgment decoding;
- structured revision architecture.

Do not force every topic into Band D. Depth must follow legal complexity.

# 3. Section-level minimums

A topic must not consist of five large headings containing one or two short sentences.

For a normal Band B/C topic:

| Section | Minimum target |
|---|---:|
| Glance | 40–80 words |
| Core explanation | 250–500 words |
| Statutory deconstruction | 400–800 words |
| Essential elements | 250–500 words |
| Application/analysis | 300–700 words |
| Procedural/practical | 250–500 words |
| Illustrations | 150–300 words each |
| Hypothetical | 400–800 words |
| Distinctions | 150–300 words per major comparison |
| Misconceptions | 5–10 meaningful traps |
| Revision points | 8–15 points |
| Q&A / problem assessment | 500–700 words |
| Written submissions | 1,000–1,500 words |

These are **minimum quality targets**, not instructions to add filler.

# 5. The "decode the judgment" student layer

A judgment should not be reduced to a one-line case summary.

For every major/landmark case, students should be able to progressively decode it:

## Level 1 — 30-second identification

Show:

- case name;
- court;
- date/year;
- citation;
- bench;
- subject;
- statutory provisions;
- one-sentence significance.

## Level 2 — 2-minute orientation

Explain:

- who the parties are;
- what triggered the dispute;
- procedural posture;
- question before the court;
- result.

## Level 3 — Material facts

Separate:

- legally material facts;
- background facts;
- disputed facts;
- admitted facts;
- facts that did not affect the decision.

Students should learn why a fact matters, not merely what happened. The ABA case-brief guide specifically emphasizes identifying legally relevant facts rather than copying every detail. citeturn0search9

## Level 4 — Procedural history

Show:

    Original proceeding
       ↓
    Trial / first forum
       ↓
    Appeal / revision
       ↓
    Current court
       ↓
    Present judgment

Explain what was decided at each important stage.

## Level 5 — Issues

Separate:

- legal issues;
- factual issues;
- procedural issues;
- constitutional issues;
- evidentiary issues.

Each issue should be written as a precise legal question.

## Level 6 — Rule / statutory framework

Identify:

- governing provision;
- definitions;
- ingredients;
- exceptions;
- provisos;
- prior authorities;
- competing rules.

## Level 7 — Arguments

Separate:

### Appellant/Petitioner/Plaintiff/Prosecution

- proposition;
- statutory basis;
- authority;
- factual basis.

### Respondent/Defendant/Accused

- proposition;
- statutory basis;
- authority;
- factual basis.

Do not invent arguments not supported by the judgment.

## Level 8 — Court reasoning

This is the most important student-learning layer.

Break reasoning into:

    Rule
      ↓
    Fact
      ↓
    Legal inference
      ↓
    Counterargument
      ↓
    Court response
      ↓
    Intermediate finding
      ↓
    Final conclusion

The ABA describes reasoning as the part that connects the legal rule to the facts. citeturn0search2

## Level 9 — Holding and ratio

Clearly distinguish:

- holding;
- ratio decidendi;
- obiter observations;
- factual findings;
- final disposition.

Do not label every judicial observation as ratio.

## Level 10 — Why the judgment matters

Explain:

- what legal position changed or was clarified;
- what earlier cases it followed;
- what cases it distinguished;
- what later cases may have affected it;
- practical significance;
- examination significance;
- litigation significance.

---

# 6. Judgment decoding must preserve source traceability

Whenever the underlying judgment is available, extracted analysis should include:

- paragraph/page reference;
- source document;
- exact citation;
- court;
- date;
- verification status.

Never invent paragraph numbers.

If the source is unavailable:

> Source text unavailable for paragraph-level verification.

Do not fabricate detailed reasoning from a case name alone.

---

# 7. Case depth targets

## Ordinary relevant case

Target:

**250–500 words**

Include:

- facts;
- issue;
- holding;
- ratio;
- relevance.

## Important authority

Target:

**500–1,000 words**

Include:

- facts;
- procedural history;
- issues;
- arguments;
- statutory framework;
- reasoning;
- holding;
- ratio;
- significance;
- later treatment where verified.

## Landmark judgment

Target:

**1,000–2,500+ words**

Include the complete 10-level judgment-decoding structure.

The target is not a replacement for the original judgment. It is a structured educational companion.

---

# 8. Do not measure quality by word count alone

A 4,000-word topic can still be poor if it contains:

- repeated sentences;
- generic filler;
- unsupported legal claims;
- duplicated paragraphs;
- fake case citations;
- generic examples;
- irrelevant history;
- no application;
- no source references.

Therefore quality gates must evaluate both:

**Depth + information density + accuracy + sourceability + educational usefulness.**

---

# 9. Content density test

Every substantive topic must pass these questions:

### Understanding

Can a first-time law student explain the concept after reading it?

### Deconstruction

Can the student identify each statutory ingredient?

### Application

Can the student apply the rule to a new fact pattern?

### Authority

Can the student identify at least one authoritative case and explain why it matters?

### Judgment reading

Can the student explain how the court moved from rule to facts to conclusion?

### Distinction

Can the student distinguish the topic from its nearest confusing neighbour?

### Practice

Can a junior lawyer identify the relevant procedural next step?

### Revision

Can the student revise the topic without rereading the entire chapter?

If any answer is “no”, increase content depth rather than merely changing formatting.

# 10. Existing-content upgrade policy

Existing short topics must not be mass-expanded with boilerplate.

Prioritize upgrades:

### Priority 1

- highYield topics;
- constitutional law;
- BNS/BNSS/BSA;
- CPC;
- Evidence/BSA;
- criminal procedure;
- major remedies;
- landmark doctrines;
- frequently searched topics.

### Priority 2

- remaining core syllabus topics;
- recurring procedural provisions;
- important commercial/family/property topics.

### Priority 3

- narrow specialist topics;
- low-frequency reference topics.

When upgrading a topic:

1. Preserve accurate existing content.
2. Remove duplicated/generic language.
3. Add missing legal layers.
4. Add authoritative sources.
5. Add case analysis.
6. Add application examples.
7. Add judgment decoding.
8. Update verification metadata.
9. Run content checks.
10. Run build/checklist.

---

# 11. No artificial uniformity

Do not force:

- every topic to exactly 3,000 words;
- every topic to exactly 10 cases;
- every topic to exactly 5 sections.

Instead use:

**minimum depth + complexity-based expansion.**

A simple definition may need 1,200 words.

A major constitutional doctrine may require 8,000 words.

A landmark judgment may require 2,000 words of educational decoding.

The author must optimize for the student's understanding, not a numeric quota.

---

# 12. Topic quality scoring rubric

Internal quality checks may score:

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---:|
| Legal accuracy | 25 |
| Source/verification | 15 |
| Doctrinal depth | 15 |
| Statutory deconstruction | 10 |
| Case-law analysis | 10 |
| Practical application | 10 |
| Student clarity | 5 |
| Judgment decoding | 5 |
| Revision usefulness | 5 |

A score should be used internally as a quality gate, not displayed to users as a legal-authority rating.

Any hallucinated authority or material legal error is an automatic failure regardless of total score.

# 14. Update policy

When updating an existing topic, do not only append another paragraph.

Review the entire topic for:

- outdated law;
- duplicated content;
- missing exceptions;
- missing procedural consequences;
- missing authorities;
- incorrect case treatment;
- weak examples;
- short reasoning sections;
- missing judgment decoding;
- missing revision points.

The result should be a coherent chapter, not a stack of patches.

---

# 15. Research benchmark

The standard is informed by established case-brief and legal-writing teaching approaches. Case-brief resources commonly include case identity, court, material facts, procedural history, issues, holdings and reasons/ratio. citeturn0search16turn0search9

Legal-writing resources emphasize that application/reasoning is not optional: it is where rules are connected to facts. citeturn0search3turn0search6

Therefore CodePackr Law should not treat a short “summary” field as equivalent to legal understanding.

---

# 16. Final standard

The goal is:

> **Every CodePackr Law topic should teach the student how to understand the law, not merely tell the student what the law says.**

And for judgments:

> **Every important judgment should be decodable from identification → facts → procedure → issues → law → arguments → reasoning → holding → ratio → significance.**

This standard applies to:

- new topics;
- existing topic upgrades;
- AI-generated topic drafts;
- subject expansion;
- case-law additions;
- judgment analysis;
- future content migration.
